# Clinical alignment review — WRHA NCD Adherence Counselling Academy
**Research date:** 11 November 2026  
**Status:** Preliminary public-guideline crosswalk — **not** clinical endorsement or formal WRHA approval.

## Scope and method
Compared the supplied **WRHA Training Proposal for CHAs in Adherence Counselling (October 2026)**, the **CHA Participant Manual (26 pages)** and **Facilitator Pack (7 pages)** against documents obtained through the Jamaican Ministry of Health & Wellness (MOHW) NCD online resource library.

The public MOHW list was checked for revised editions. The 2024 diabetes and hypertension guideline covers, 2025 asthma cover, 2024 screening guideline, December 2025 exercise SOP and August 2019 nutrition protocol were examined. Other publications exist and WRHA may hold newer unpublished circulars; their absence from the public library **does not prove** there are no newer internal instructions.

## The primary clinical references that should control content
1. [Hypertension clinical management, October 2024 — official PDF](https://ncdip.moh.gov.jm/download/4262/) — 65 pp. Replaces earlier interim guidance as the primary public reference.
2. [Type 2 diabetes clinical management, December 2024 — official PDF](https://ncdip.moh.gov.jm/download/4259/) — 47 pp. Appendix A includes a diabetes/hypertension primary-care consultation form with medication and nutrition adherence fields.
3. [National NCD screening guidelines, 2nd ed., updated 2024 — official PDF](https://ncdip.moh.gov.jm/download/4283/) — 111 pp. Published June 2024 (following March 2024 update); publicly launched in July 2025.
4. [Asthma clinical management pocket guide, March 2025 — official PDF](https://ncdip.moh.gov.jm/download/4291/) — 60 pp. Explicitly includes inhaler technique and medication adherence.
5. [NCD screening pocket guide — official PDF](https://ncdip.moh.gov.jm/download/4288/) — 23 pp. Companion reference.
6. [Exercise prescription SOP, December 2025 — official PDF](https://ncdip.moh.gov.jm/download/4297/) — 12 pp. Intended for **medical practitioners**, NOT independent prescribing by Community Health Aides.
7. [Nutrition management protocol, August 2019 — official PDF](https://ncdip.moh.gov.jm/download/2374/) — 177 pp. Useful for multidisciplinary context; **do not use its older BP classification or treatment tables in place of the newer 2024 guidelines**.

The MOHW resources pages also retain **2020 interim** diabetes and hypertension materials. Those are older than the 2024 clinical guidelines and should not be treated as the current primary documents.

## Nine-module alignment check

| WRHA module | Assessment | Clinical / curriculum safeguard |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Understanding NCDs | **Generally aligned** | Major conditions and consequences match MOHW subject areas. Explicitly date prevalence statistics from JHLS III (2016–17) rather than implying they were measured in 2026. Include updated 2024/2025 references. |
| 2. What Is Adherence? | **Generally aligned** | Person-centred partnership is consistent with modern chronic care; terminology of compliance/adherence/concordance is the WRHA teaching framework, not a new MOHW clinical prescribing protocol. |
| 3. Why Don't People Adhere? | **Generally aligned** | Barriers are relevant, but client/treatment/social/health-system categories are a pedagogical organising framework. Handle reported side effects and possible hypoglycaemia through referral rather than medication advice. |
| 4. Communication Skills | **Strong pedagogical fit** | Active listening, empathy and non-judgmental questions support chronic care and the related MOHW/C-TECH Chronic Care Model course. Evaluated as training skills, not as national prescribing rules. |
| 5. Motivational Interviewing | **Strong pedagogical fit** | OARS, confidence rulers and Ask–Share–Ask are useful communication practices. Have a qualified facilitator review examples and scoring. |
| 6. The 5 A's & Assessment | **Generally aligned; programme-specific** | The WRHA manual uses **Ask → Assess → Advise → Agree → Assist**. Present this as the **WRHA-proposed framework**, pending training-team adoption; do not assert that all MOHW clinical manuals mandate this exact sequence. Clinical medication decisions stay with nurses/medical officers/pharmacists. |
| 7. Scope, Confidentiality & Danger Signs | **Aligned in principle; safety review essential** | Urgent signs such as focal neurological deficits, serious chest pain or severe breathing difficulty require immediate escalation per WRHA protocols. A new/nonhealing diabetic foot wound needs **prompt clinician triage**; do not assume it can wait for an ordinary future clinic. Validate emergency-contact numbers, roles and escalation timelines with WRHA. |
| 8. Supporting Self-Management at Home | **Generally aligned; safeguards required** | Teach-back, reminder routines and supported referrals are appropriate. Patients' medication organisers/storage should be reviewed with a pharmacist; personalised inhaler instructions and exercise prescriptions are clinician-led. The December 2025 exercise SOP is aimed at medical practitioners. The 2019 nutrition protocol is supporting, not the latest BP/drug standard. |
| 9. Referral, Documentation & Follow-Up | **Aligned in principle; operational details need local sign-off** | Recording objective reports, agreed plans and referral outcomes is consistent with continuity of care. Specific timelines (e.g., one week) and criteria for when a referral is “complete” need to follow WRHA procedures. |

## Items that should be confirmed before publication
1. **Current WRHA emergency and referral protocols:** Validate the participant manual's emergency numbers, diabetic foot wound escalation, low/high glucose response, suspected stroke, chest pain, breathlessness and suicidal crisis instructions. Do not replace local protocol with an automatically generated algorithm.
2. **CHA scope of practice:** Verify exactly which BP/glucose testing, teaching, medication review and referral activities are authorised for CHAs in the four parishes. Explain that clinical guidelines include tasks reserved for clinicians.
3. **Assessment and certification rules:** The facilitator pack proposes **30/40 as a suggested skills threshold only**, and explicitly asks the training team to approve it. The programme has not provided a final knowledge pass mark. Do not use prototype browser practice scores to issue WRHA certificates.
4. **Programme schedule:** The October proposal describes a one-day November workshop; the November 10 email changes this to online Modules 1–3 (December 10), in-person Modules 4–7 (January), then online Modules 8–9. Update the manuals and Moodle sequence accordingly.
5. **Confidentiality and case exercises:** Use invented situations in the public app. Real patient identifiers, course grades, authorised assessments and staff data belong only in approved institutional systems.
6. **Internal publications:** Ask Dr. Johnson-Campbell and WRHA NCD leadership to confirm whether newer internal MOHW/WRHA circulars or local SOPs supersede any public documents.

## Specific evidence notes
- The **October 2024 hypertension guideline** addresses medication adherence, follow-up and warning signs and contains recommendations for clinician assessment of hypertensive emergencies and specialist referral.
- The **December 2024 diabetes guideline** addresses treatment adherence, risk of hypoglycaemia, clinician-directed self-monitoring and foot complications.
- The **March 2025 asthma guideline** emphasises inhaler technique and adherence, making asthma a worthwhile supportive example for CHAs.
- The **December 2025 exercise SOP** identifies medical practitioners as its users; it is not a CHA prescription authority.
- The **June 2024 / 2nd-edition screening guideline** lists CHAs among members of the primary health-care audience, but each profession must work within its separate authorised scope.

**Bottom line:** The WRHA course has a strong foundation for adherence counselling, **not** a fully validated collection of national clinical instructions. Keep the current 2024/2025 Jamaican guidelines as primary references, with clinician-led review and local operational sign-off before rollout.
